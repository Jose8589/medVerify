Your surgeon says the board certification is current. Here's how to actually confirm that.

"Board certified" gets used like it's one credential. In Mexico's system it's actually three separate records, issued by three different institutions — and only one of them has an expiration date. Here's what each one means, and how to check all three before you book.

Ask a clinic if the surgeon is board certified, and you'll almost always hear yes. It's rarely a lie. It's just rarely the whole answer. In the U.S., that phrase points to one credential, checked in one database. In Mexico, it points to three — issued by three separate federal systems, cross-checked by none of them automatically.

None of that makes a doctor unsafe on its own. It means the phrase doesn't tell you what you think it tells you — and the gap matters most for exactly the specialties patients travel abroad for.

A current specialty license and a current CONACEM certification are not the same record. A doctor can hold one without the other, and nothing on a clinic's website is required to say so.

Three credentials, three different questions

Each one answers something different. None of them, alone, answers all of it.

01

Cédula profesional

Did they finish medical school and get authorized to practice medicine at all? Every licensed doctor in Mexico has one — it's the baseline, not the specialty.

02

Cédula de especialidad

Did they complete a recognized residency in the specialty they're offering you? This confirms the training happened — once.

03

CONACEM certification

Is that specialty training still considered current, as of today? This is the one credential with an expiration date — and the one most often left unchecked.

Frequently asked questions

Can a doctor be legally licensed but not actually board-certified in the specialty they're practicing?+
Yes. A cédula de especialidad confirms the residency happened. It doesn't confirm the CONACEM certification tied to it is still current — the two are checked in different systems, so it's possible to have one without the other.
Does an expired CONACEM certification mean the doctor is unsafe?+
Not by itself. Some doctors let a renewal lapse for administrative reasons and remain highly competent. What it does mean is that the specific claim "board certified" hasn't been independently reviewed recently — worth knowing before surgery, not after.
Can I check this myself before booking?+
You can. Both registries are public. The friction is that they're Spanish-only, organized by internal specialty codes rather than names, and don't cross-reference each other. That gap is what a review like this closes.
